Faction, power, and moderation in Hume

Supervisor: Prof. Amy Schmitter

My thesis examines David Hume’s political philosophy through the interconnected themes of faction, power, and moderation. Drawing on his “science of man,” I develop Hume as a proto-social scientist keenly attentive to how power operates in society and to the consequences of factionalism. Reading him against Hobbes, I treat Hume as a theorist of power who grounds authority in effective power rather than legitimate right. Though he is often construed as a philosopher of moderation, my work highlights the central importance of conflict in his politics, offering a historically sensitive lens on authority, social hierarchy, and the management of division.

Earlier work

My MA thesis, “The Homo Oeconomicus’s Sympathy: A Reading of Adam Smith’s Theory of Moral Sentiments in Light of Michel Foucault’s Concept of Liberal Governmentality” (UFPE, 2019, supervised by Prof. Filipe Campello), began a line of research on Smith, Foucault, and the figure of homo oeconomicus that I have continued in several conference papers. I also work on the theory of sympathy in Hume and Smith.
